How FairParlay prices a parlay

Fair probability. Every two-way market (over/under, home/away, cover/not) is quoted by each book with a built-in margin. We remove it book by book, then average across books with Pinnacle — the sharpest book — counted three times. One-sided "anytime TD" prices are corrected with a mapping fitted on 3,863 historical TD prices; the books kept about 9% on those.

Correlation. Legs from different games are independent, so their probabilities multiply. Legs from the same game are not: a quarterback's passing yards move with his receivers' yards and with the game total. We estimated those relationships from every NFL game since 2023 and price the parlay with a Gaussian copula — the same device the books use. The "independent" figure shows what naive multiplication would say.

The book's edge. A parlay's payout implies a probability (1 ÷ decimal odds). Compare it with the fair probability and you get your expected return per dollar, which is negative at most books on most parlays — that is the business model of parlays. This tool finds the least negative one, and the occasional positive one, mostly in player props where books disagree with each other.

Same-game parlays. Books quote SGPs with their own correlation model, so the "pays" column for a same-game ticket is straight multiplication — what the legs would pay as independent bets. Paste the quote your betting app shows to see the real edge on it.

Kalshi appears as a price source on moneylines (its ask plus the trading fee). Kalshi prices multi-leg tickets in its own app; paste that quote too.

Prices are snapshots and move; this is information, not a recommendation, and FairParlay takes no bets. 21+. If betting stops being fun, call 1-800-GAMBLER.
